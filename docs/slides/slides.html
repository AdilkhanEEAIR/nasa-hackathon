The problem

Earth science reaches people almost only through the eyes

  • NASA's Earth Information Center tells the story of a changing planet with stunning visualizations, and only visualizations.
  • Globally, at least 2.2 billion people have a near or distance vision impairment (WHO, World report on vision, 2019). For many of them a map is silent.
  • Most sonification projects play a picture line by line. You hear a melody but not where things are.

Our question:

Can you navigate an Earth visualization by ear and by touch, and understand where the planet is bright, smoky, stormy, warm or cold?

The solution

A sonar for Earth data: the shrinking ring

↑ High notescome from the north, low notes from the south
→ Right earis east, left ear is west
◉ Loudermeans more (light, dust, storms…)
♪ Timbresays what it is: each phenomenon has its own voice
  • Pick a place. 16 voices on a ring start up to 4,000 km away and close in on it. At the end the ring meets in one note: the value at the centre.
  • The ring is computed on the sphere (great-circle distance), so it is true on Earth at any zoom.
  • Explore mode: walk a probe with the arrow keys. A voice names the place: "110 km south of Bishkek".
  • Play all years: 2012→2023 as a melody. Save scan as WAV: build your own Earth playlist.

How it sounds

One rule, a different voice for every phenomenon

TrackPhenomenonVoice
01 Night Lights 2012–2023City lightsWarm, twinkling tone
02 What ChangedMore / less / same lightHigh sparkle / low buzz / soft sine
03 AerosolsSmoke · dust · sea salt · sulfateCrackle · dry rustle · surf · buzz
04 Lightning (ISS)New flash · storm densitySharp crack · thunder rumble
05 Ocean TemperatureCold → warm waterDull dark tone → bright rich tone

Major pentatonic scale, so any combination stays musical. Loudness is matched across tracks (RMS 0.07–0.12, no clipping).

NASA data, read honestly

We sonify the rendered EIC visualizations, by fixed rules

NASA SVS visualizationIDWhat we read
Change in Night Lights 2012–2023 (EIC version), Black Marble5313Brightness per year · change-map hue (purple / orange / white)
GEOS Aerosols, 19 Aug 20255669Nearest legend colour → 4 types; no sea salt on land; city dots removed
Lightning Events Detected from the ISS 2017–20235011Yellow dots per video frame; new dots = strikes; official dates (a "2003" typo fixed)
Global Sea Surface Currents and Temperature (ECCO2)3912Hue matched to the 0–32 °C colorbar (round-trip error ≤ 1.5 °C)
  • Fixed rules, never normalised per frame. Frame indices, crops and georeferencing are measured from the pixels (docs/DATA_NOTES.md).
  • Stated limits: the aerosol legend has no amounts · lightning only within ±52° (silence = no data) · the ocean frame is one day, not a trend.

Accessibility and hardware

Keyboard only, screen-reader ready, and you can feel it

  • No mouse needed: Space play · arrows move · PgUp/PgDn year · W where am I · E explore · T tutorial.
  • Labelled controls, a live status region, a spoken guide (can be turned off for screen readers), a 10-second sound tutorial.
  • High contrast, large targets, keyboard hints always on screen, prefers-reduced-motion respected.
  • Fast for everyone: first screen 0.14 MB, ready in under 1.5 s on slow 4G.

Tactile controller (Arduino, USB, Web Serial)

  • Joystick moves the centre · knob picks the year · button plays.
  • 4 vibration motors N / E / S / W: a tactile compass driven by the same ring. Lightning strikes are jolts on their side.
  • Open protocol and firmware. Android phones can vibrate instead.
