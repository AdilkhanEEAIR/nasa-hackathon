Problem

Earth science reaches people almost only through the eyes.

2.2 billion

people, at least, have a near or distance vision impairment.

Source: World Health Organization, fact sheet "Blindness and vision impairment", who.int

  • Who it affects: for them, a map of night lights, smoke or storms is silent.
  • Why now: NASA's Earth Information Center tells the story of a changing planet in pictures, and the challenge asks for a second sense.
  • The gap: playing a picture line by line gives a melody, not where things are.

Solution

A ring of sound closes in on a place you choose: the map becomes a compass you can hear.

High notescome from the north, low notes from the south
Right earis east, left ear is west
Loudermeans more
Timbresays what it is: one voice per phenomenon
  • What is new: direction and distance around your point, not a left-to-right line. The same values drive vibration.
  • Why it works: one rule on all eight tracks, fixed scales, and a pentatonic scale so any data sounds musical.

How it works

Pixels of NASA frames become sound, live in the browser, by fixed rules.

NASA SVS visualizationWe read
Change in Night Lights 2012–2023, EIC Version (5313)brightness, 12 years
GEOS Aerosols (5669)4 particle types by legend colour
Lightning Events Detected from the ISS (5011)flashes in every video frame
Global Sea Surface Currents and Temperature (3912)0–32 °C from the colour bar
GEOS-FP Near-Surface Wind Speed (5148)3.6–34 m/s from the colour bar
GEDI Forest Height (4950)green in the picture (Visual)
DYAMOND Global Carbon Dioxide (5196)plume colour (Visual)
A 3D View of an Atmospheric River (4960)vapour blue (Visual)

Method. 16 voices on a ring, laid on the sphere, read the frame 30 times a second. Direction → pitch and stereo, value → loudness, phenomenon → instrument. Globe videos use a 12-voice needle.

Honest classes. 5 tracks are Data-true (values read from the colour scale); 3 are Visual and say so.

AI tools. Claude (Anthropic) wrote code and drafted docs under the team's direction and review. No AI-generated images, video or audio.

Stack. Static site: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Web Audio, Canvas. Python, NumPy, Pillow, FFmpeg for media. No libraries, no server.

Impact and next steps

A map anyone can listen to, on the device they already have.

Who benefits

Blind and low-vision people first. Science centres, schools, and every listener who wants a new way to notice patterns.

Reach

One web page, phone or laptop. Keyboard and screen reader, voice guide, large text, phone vibration, controller rumble.

Next

Listening tests with blind and low-vision users. Haptics on real devices. More EIC visualizations. Translations.

Stated openly: we sonify the rendered visualizations, not raw measurements. No tests with blind users have been run yet, so we quote no results.
